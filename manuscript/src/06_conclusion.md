# 6. Conclusions

Public reanalysis data allow a reproducible, long-term estimate of the
energy yield at the grid node nearest the Gulf of Saros candidate area:
about {{energy_yield:net.gwh_per_mw|.2f}} GWh MW⁻¹ yr⁻¹ for 1994–2024 with the
reference turbine and the assumed losses. The more consequential findings
concern how far such an estimate can be trusted for screening.

First, the narrow sampling confidence interval of a long-term mean is a poor
guide to how well the resource is known. A neighbouring grid node, the other
wind data product and the alternative loss assumptions each move the result
by several times its half-width, and the uncertainty of the power curve and
of the choice of turbine, which was not quantified, comes in addition.
Reporting the sampling confidence interval alone would overstate the
confidence that the data support.

Second, coastal reanalysis has specific limitations for an area of this
size. The candidate polygon is narrower than the resolution of the
reanalysis and contains no grid node, and the model's representation of the
nearest node is influenced by land. Neighbouring nodes differ
systematically, and a finer
product driven by the same reanalysis gives a clearly higher wind speed.
These differences cannot be resolved from model data, and they are not
reduced by a longer record.

Third, a record of about a decade is too short to stand for the long-term
mean. The most recent 11-year window is the highest in the record,
{{period_sensitivity:periods.2014-2024.energy_change_vs_reference_pct|.1f}} % above the 31-year mean, a difference of the same size as the
sampling uncertainty of an 11-year mean. The reference period should
therefore be stated and tested.

For screening studies of coastal candidate areas, these results support
three practices, none of them new: state and test the reference period,
report the sensitivity to the choice of grid node and wind product, and
keep the sampling confidence interval, the sensitivity experiments and the assumptions apart
instead of merging them into one interval. For the Gulf of Saros candidate
area, the screening further shows water depths below
{{site_screening:bathymetry.depth_m.max|.0f}} m throughout and an
approximate overlap of about
{{site_screening:protected_area_overlap.saros_ock_2010.overlap_share_pct|.0f}} %
with a Special Environmental Protection Area, which raises a question for
the competent authorities. The estimates presented here are preliminary
and model-based. Wind measurements inside the candidate polygon are the
step that would most improve knowledge of its resource.
