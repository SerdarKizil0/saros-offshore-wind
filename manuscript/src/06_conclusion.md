# 6. Conclusions

Public reanalysis data allow a reproducible, long-term estimate of the
energy yield at the grid node nearest the Gulf of Saros candidate area:
{{energy_yield:net.gwh_per_mw|.3f}} GWh MW⁻¹ yr⁻¹ for 1994–2024 with the
reference turbine and the assumed losses. The more consequential findings
concern how far such an estimate can be trusted for screening.

First, the narrow sampling interval of a long-term mean is a poor guide to
how well the resource is known. In this case it is the smallest of the
categories examined: a short recent reference period, a neighbouring grid
cell, the other wind data product and the alternative loss assumptions can
each move the result by more than the half-width of that interval. Reporting the sampling interval alone would overstate the
confidence that the data support.

Second, coastal reanalysis has specific limitations for an area of this
size. The candidate polygon is narrower than the reanalysis grid spacing and
contains no grid node, and the nearest node belongs to a cell that the model
treats as partly land. Neighbouring cells differ systematically, and a finer
product driven by the same reanalysis gives a clearly higher wind speed.
These differences cannot be resolved from model data, and they are not
reduced by a longer record.

Third, a recent decade is not a neutral choice of reference period at this
location. The most recent 11-year window is the highest in the record, so an
estimate based on recent years alone is biased high relative to the
long-term mean of the same series.

For screening studies of coastal candidate areas, these results support
three practices: state and test the reference period, report the
sensitivity to the choice of grid cell and wind product, and keep the
sampling interval, the sensitivity experiments and the assumptions apart
instead of merging them into one interval. For the Gulf of Saros candidate
area, the screening further shows water depths below
{{site_screening:bathymetry.depth_m.max|.0f}} m throughout and an
approximate overlap of about
{{site_screening:protected_area_overlap.saros_ock_2010.overlap_share_pct|.0f}} %
with a Special Environmental Protection Area, which requires clarification
by the competent authorities. The estimates presented here are preliminary
and model-based. Wind measurements inside the candidate polygon are the
step that would most improve knowledge of its resource.
